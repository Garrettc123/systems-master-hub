# QIH Framework — Intellectual Foundations

The following are structural parallels and sources of inspiration, not claims of equivalence, proof, endorsement, or scientific validation. QIH applies ideas from control, information, complexity, and systems theory to the engineering problem of building auditable autonomous decision systems.

| Thinker or tradition | Relevant idea | QIH engineering parallel |
|---|---|---|
| Ilya Prigogine | Ordered systems far from equilibrium require continual throughput and maintenance | Maintenance loops are treated as ongoing operating functions, not optional afterthoughts |
| Claude Shannon | Information can be represented, measured, and transmitted under formal constraints | Typed inputs, evidence records, and decision summaries reduce ambiguity across system stages |
| Alan Turing | Computation can be expressed through formal state transitions | QIH focuses on explicit transitions from signal to decision to committed action |
| Norbert Wiener | Feedback supports purposeful control in changing environments | Outcome data and monitoring inform controlled adjustments to thresholds and policies |
| W. Ross Ashby | A regulator must have sufficient response variety for the system it governs | Systems need adequate routing, policy, escalation, and safe-failure responses for their environment |
| Karl Friston | Adaptive systems update internal models in response to prediction error | QIH uses calibration and drift review as an engineering analogue, not a claim about biology |
| Andrei Kolmogorov | Compression and description length provide a lens on structure and complexity | Historical outcomes can be summarized into reusable operational rules and patterns |
| Gregory Bateson | Intelligence and meaning emerge through relationships and context | QIH tracks relationships among signals, decisions, outcomes, and feedback instead of treating events as isolated |
| Douglas Hofstadter | Self-reference and feedback can create higher-order loops | QIH distinguishes first-order action logic from second-order oversight of that logic |
| Nassim Nicholas Taleb | Some systems improve through carefully managed exposure to stress and error | Controlled anomaly review and remediation can make operations more resilient; this is not a guarantee of antifragility |
| Kurt Gödel | Formal systems have inherent limits in self-proof and completeness | QIH requires explicit uncertainty, escalation, review boundaries, and external verification rather than claims of perfect self-validation |

## Interpretive boundary

These analogies are conceptual. They do not mean that QIH reproduces thermodynamics, proves information-theoretic optimality, implements a Turing-complete theory of governance, or validates neuroscience, physics, or mathematics beyond its software-engineering scope.

## Why the synthesis matters

Autonomous systems are simultaneously information-processing, feedback-driven, stateful, and accountable to real organizations. QIH combines useful design constraints from several disciplines into an operational question: can a responsible reviewer reconstruct what happened, assess why it happened, and improve the system without losing traceability?
